1. Summary and recommendation

Current goal Where we stand. On our own held-out recording sessions (2-fold CV by session, 181 throw motions = 77 throws + 104 feints), the best models detect:

  • Old banks + false-alarm recipe (r2_old_fa): 106 of 181 at 2.0 false alarms/min on our idle footage, about 390 ms before release. In the 7 ordinary sessions it gets 98 of 107. Almost all misses are in two sessions: 215305 (key-press labels, 6 of 64) and d01 (ball invisible, 2 of 10).
  • Matched external positives + false-alarm recipe (r2_ext_fa): 79 of 181 with 0 false alarms in 2 minutes of held-out idle (88 of 181 at 1.5/min). It detects later (median 80–235 ms before release) and also catches 46–55 % of DailyDVS throws by people it has never seen.

For labelling our next recordings, use final_old_fa.pt at threshold 0.95. It is clearly better in ordinary sessions (98/107 vs. 72/107) and detects earlier. Switch to final_ext_fa.pt (threshold 0.90–0.95) where people walk through the scene and false alarms matter more than recall. That is the one case where the old-bank model cannot get below 3 false alarms/min.

What today's work established

  1. "Our throws are different from the dataset throws" was a preprocessing artefact. With the old DailyDVS import, a logistic regression told DailyDVS clips from ours with 90 % balanced accuracy, and our models detected 0 of 590 DailyDVS throws. After sensor matching, the same probe is at chance (0.52–0.55). A model trained only on our positives already detects 59–92 of 366 matched DailyDVS throws. Training on them reaches 169–242 of 366.
  2. The false-alarm recipe was the biggest single gain. Positive weight 1.5 instead of 3, 45 % own idle in the negative mix, and light augmentation. With it, both bank versions reach ≤ 2 false alarms/min on our idle stream. The previous recipe could not get below 3/min at any threshold.
  3. Mixing external positives into training costs own recall at this model size. At 40 % of the positives the network spends capacity on DailyDVS throws and fires on DailyDVS arm actions (10–20 FA/min). At 20 % (r3) it is no better.
  4. Video converted with v2e from 30 fps HMDB51 is still recognisable as video (probe 0.73–0.94). Events bunch at the video frames, and interpolation does not fix that. It was not used for training.

3. Board format and model (shared by all work)

  • Input: binary [T, 2, 80, 80], 10 ms frames. A cell is 1 if any event of that polarity fell into its 4×4 block of the 320×320 sensor (despot downsampling: 4, frequency: 100).
  • Network: riverops SpikingConvBN2d stack 16-32-64-64 (stride 2, 80→5), PLIF neurons, conv head [T, K, 1, 1]. About 63k weights with a 1-output head. Only conv-LIF layers, because the exporter supports nothing else.
  • Pretraining (training/pretrain/, Tim branch): DVS Gesture, 30 epochs, held-out users 24–29: 87.9 % per 0.64 s window, 91.3 % per recording. Checkpoint training/pretrain/checkpoints/dvsg_best.pt; on the A100 the early trainer initialises from ~/snn_pretrain/runs/sweep_v1/best.pt.
  • Export: fuse, then export_for_river, giving model.json, weights.txt and config.yaml (training/export_snn.py on data-pipeline, throwdet/export.py on throw-detector). QAT accuracy has not been measured.
  • Live readout Current goal: host side, y_t = α·y_{t−1} + (1−α)·spike_t with α = 0.85 (about 65 ms). An alarm is a crossing of threshold θ, followed by 1 s refractory. throwdet/live_readout.py uses the two-neuron variant with a hold-frames rule.

4. Earlier goal Real vs. fake

The task was to decide, after the swing, whether the ball really left the hand. None of this is used by the current goal except the infrastructure (bank format, pretrained backbone, shortcut probes, session-grouped CV).

4.1 data-pipeline branch: dataset, generators, simulator

Commits 3d2054a…ee0e2da. tools/build_dataset.py made auto labels for the 25 recordings of dataset/01: 13 throw, 11 feint, 1 invalid. A frame-by-frame review agreed with the arm-motion labels but found the yarn ball invisible to the sensor in every clip.

Generated training sets in dataset/build/:

  • aug_train: real windows augmented (speed, mirror, scale, shift, rotation, thinning, shake, distractor person, noise, refractory, time jitter).
  • splice_train: throw wind-up cross-faded into a feint's return phase.
  • synth_train: procedural 3-D arm renderer plus our own event simulator tools/event_sim.py, v2 and v3.
  • video_train: Wikimedia phone videos through tools/video_to_events.py.

  • cut-and-reverse feints: 100 %
  • splice feints: 95.5 %, then 84.7 % after fixes
  • aug throws / feints: 62 / 70 %
  • synth v2 throws / feints: 83 / 86 %; synth v3: 82–96 %

Synthetic from scratch does not transfer. training/pretrain/runs_throw/summary.json, 38 real windows, 3-class:

  • 5-fold CV on real data: 0.76 (CI 0.62–0.89); throw vs. feint 0.78.
  • Train on synth, test on real: 0.18–0.37; throw vs. feint 0.00–0.26.
  • Firing rates differ between real and synth already at the first layer (eval_model_*.json), and synth clips have about half the event rate of real ones (eval_input_stats.json).

4.2 experiments/robust_release: fighting early wrong fitting (Tim and data-pipeline branches)

Diagnosis on the 24 old recordings. Leave-one-recording-out logistic regression gets 0.75 from the active-cell count in the first 100 ms and 0.92 from an 8×8 activity map 300–200 ms before release, but only 0.54 after release. The label was predictable from the prefix, for two reasons: block recording, and an auto-label rule that used pre-release activity.

Method (robust_cv.py):

  • Windows cut on the fly from full recordings.
  • Generation from real data only:
    • thin: global event thinning.
    • swap: context before a cut 120–450 ms pre-release comes from another recording.
    • overlay: OR-in a thinned, shifted chunk of idle.
    • mix: EventMix.
  • Per-frame loss after release + 50 ms.
  • Prefix symmetry --sym: before release, the throw and feint logits must be equal.
  • AdamW, EMA weights with BN re-estimation, no checkpoint selection.
  • Shortcut metrics: prefix_tf@−200ms should be about 0.5; swap_tf should equal accuracy.

5. Current goal The throw-detector branch (throwdet/)

A parallel line of work on the same question: is a throw motion happening right now? It is not run on today's banks, and its metrics differ from section 8 (clip level, window −250…+300 ms), so compare trends, not numbers.

  • Data (5,615 clips, prep.py):
    • own 181 throw motions + 543 idle
    • own_aug / splice: 423 + 108
    • synth v2 + video: 832 + 416
    • DailyDVS via the old importer: 590 throw-like positives + 2,522 negatives
  • Countermeasures:
    • balanced sampling per (domain, label)
    • DailyDVS thinned to our density
    • our idle OR-ed under external clips
    • mirror, scale, shift, speed, dropout
    A logistic regression still told DailyDVS from our camera with 93 % accuracy.
  • Results (4-fold by recording, 724 own clips, readout tuned per false-alarm budget):
    • e10 (own negatives weighted up): AUC 0.865–0.924, recall 0.54–0.79 at 2 FA/min.
    • e26 (twice the DailyDVS share): AUC 0.88–0.92, recall 0.58–0.82 at 2 FA/min.
    • Without synth/video: AUC 0.83–0.85.
    • Activity baseline: 0.77 balanced accuracy.
    • Seed variance: up to 0.06 AUC and 0.25 recall.
  • Replay of the 01 stream (55 s, unseen by model D): 23 of 25 swings detected, 3–4 other alarms, alarm about 150 ms before release.
  • Deliverables: models A–E with readout_fa{1,2,4}.json, live_readout.py, replay.py, adapt.sh (site fine-tuning in 5–10 min).

Relevance today: its DailyDVS positives came through the old importer, so they carried the "DailyDVS look" shortcut that section 7 removes. Its readout (two neurons, difference, hold frames) gives more control over false alarms than the threshold-only rule used in section 8.

6. Current goal early_cv iteration 1 (previous session)

experiments/early_v1/ (untracked), A100 ~/early/runs_early/. One output neuron, training samples are 3 s streams stitched from clips (positive with p = 0.5, release at a random position; negatives from noah-idle 0.3, mb 0.25, dd 0.25, dvsg 0.2; 15 % quiet gaps).

Target: 1 from R − onset to R + 300 ms, ignored for dc before that, 0 elsewhere. Loss: weighted MSE on the EMA readout (positive weight 3, early ×2). PLIF τ capped at 80 ms so nothing integrates for minutes.

Anti-shortcut set: density normalisation to 80–500 cells/frame, a foreign "bed" layer under 70 % of streams, polarity swap, flip / shift / zoom, time stretch, random clip boundaries, noise and hot pixels.

Evaluation per held-out fold: 60 s negative stream per source (FA/min with 1 s refractory), a 60 s noise-only drift stream, and each held-out positive after 3 s of held-out negatives (detection within −800…+300 ms). Four folds, 800 steps, all at θ = 0.95:

7.2 Fetching

  • DailyDVS (fetch_dailydvs.py): reads the 91 GB zip's central directory with HTTP range requests on the HF mirror Charlieqaq/DailyDVS-200 and fetches only the selected members as raw events (no crop, no anchor). 3,114 recordings, about 10 GB, about 15 min on the A100; 32 lost to HTTP 429 rate limits. Recordings with Move = 1 (camera moving) are skipped, which leaves 2,063.
  • HMDB51 (fetch_hmdb.py): HF mirror divm/hmdb51, one mp4 per clip, 4,122 clips / 516 MB for the selected classes. The tags (camera motion, view, body, quality, number of people) go into the manifest; the CV group is the source video. On the A100 the download hit rate limits, so the laptop copy was uploaded instead.

7.3 Video to events with v2e (v2e_convert.py)

  • Simulator: v2e 1.8.5 (pip install v2e, needs Python 3.14). SuperSloMo frame interpolation (checkpoint SuperSloMo39.ckpt, auto-downloaded) plus the v2e DVS pixel model. Per clip: threshold 0.15–0.25 (±10 % ON/OFF), mismatch σ 0.02–0.05, photoreceptor cutoff 0.25–0.9 × fout/2π (keeps the IIR stable), refractory 0.1–1 ms, no leak or shot noise.
  • Camera motion: LK feature tracking + RANSAC similarity fit; reject if background p90 > 0.5 px/frame. Calibrated on the HMDB tags: keeps 72 % of "static" and 10 % of "moving" clips. Phase correlation and border-change tests rejected everything and were dropped.
  • Crop and window: square crop of the full frame height around the motion (no padding). Window from motion-energy peak −1.1 s to +0.8 s, including a 0.2 s pre-roll for the pixel model.
  • Cost: SuperSloMo takes about 500 s per clip on the laptop CPU and about 3.3 s per clip on the A100 (6 parallel). --interp dis (OpenCV DIS optical flow) is a CPU fallback at about 2.5 s per clip.
  • Yield: 46 of 102 throw videos are usable (46 camera moves, 10 slow-motion or too short). 82 negative clips from 15 per class.

7.4 Sensor matching (match.py), in this order

  1. Foreign noise. Background-activity filter (≥ 2 other events within ±1 px, ±3 ms bins), then cells with fewer than 2 active neighbours (3×3, ±1 frame) are removed. Without this, DVXplorer noise drew the source field of view as a visible rectangle.
  2. Actor mask. Activity map smoothed 7×7 cells, above 8 % of its maximum, largest component, grown by 2 cells. Only the actor is taken from foreign data. Positives with less than 75 % of their activity on one actor are dropped (distant or diffuse recordings: 546 → 366 DailyDVS positive clips).
  3. Anchor. Event-rate peak (20 ms bins, 3-tap smoothing) becomes release = 700 ms in a 1.2 s clip, the same rule as our labels. Negatives are anchored the same way (their busiest moment), plus one random window per negative (--neg-random).
  4. Geometry. Motion height (5–95 % of active cells, −500…+300 ms) scaled to a sampled own value ×U(0.85, 1.15), clamp 0.3–2.5. Centre moved to a sampled own centre ±6/±3 cells. Sub-pixel jitter when enlarging. Source frame edges inside the image are faded out over 12 % of the source size. A first version that moved cut edges to the bottom border shifted everything down (cy 51 vs. our 41) and was replaced.
  5. Polarity. ON share of cells moved to a sampled own value by raising the dominant polarity's threshold (bisection). DVXplorer is ON-heavy: 0.55 against our 0.48. Doing this before the density step mattered: in the other order density undershot by 25 %.
  6. Density. Active cells per frame in the swing window matched to sampled own ×U(0.85, 1.2) by a per-pixel threshold increase: keep every k-th event of a pixel and polarity, fractional k, random phase, bisection. This thins weak edges first; random dropout would leave speckle.
  7. Noise. Our sensor's per-cell noise map (from the 92 empty-scene idle clips, including hot cells, mean 0.4 cells/frame) is added with gain exp(N(0.7, 0.5)).
  8. Video only: --time-jitter-us 33333 (uniform, one frame period).

Outputs: .bin clips in our format plus labels.csv in our schema, with src, role, cls, group (person / video), variant, all parameters and stats. to_bank.py writes the early_cv bank, with amb clips in a separate *_amb.npz. DailyDVS: 3 variants per positive, 1 + 1 random per negative.

8.1 What the runs show

  • Sensor matching makes external throws learnable (x_old 4/590 → x_ext_light 242/366). Even without training on them, x_own_light detects 59–92 of 366. Our throws and DailyDVS throws share the motion once the sensor differences are gone.
  • With the matched data the task gets honestly harder. Final training loss 0.07–0.10 against 0.015–0.05 on the old banks. "Foreign look = negative" no longer works as a shortcut.
  • Light augmentation beats the full anti-shortcut set (x_ext_light vs. x_ext), as iteration 1 suggested. Once preprocessing matches the sensors, the heavy noise and density normalisation only hurt.
  • The FA recipe is the most effective lever for our idle stream. x_old 6.0 → r2_old_fa 2.0 FA/min at θ 0.95 with the same ordinary-session recall (98/107), and motion_buddy 8.5 → 1.5.
  • Old vs. ext banks under the FA recipe.
    • Old: better recall in ordinary sessions (98 vs. 72 of 107) and earlier (−390 vs. −80 ms).
    • Ext: quieter on the busy half of our idle (fold 0, with people walking). r2_ext_fa has 0 alarms from θ 0.95; r2_old_fa stays at 3/min even at θ 0.99.
    • Ext also generalises to other people's throws.
  • Every model fires on DailyDVS arm actions once it has seen DailyDVS throws (punch, wave, point: 9–21 FA/min). These matched clips are honest hard negatives, so this is a real weakness that the old banks hid behind the domain shortcut.
  • Sessions 215305 and d01 are near 0 for every model. The labels there (key presses, invisible ball) and the recording conditions are the likely cause, not the model.

11. Caveats

  • Small evaluation. Own-idle FA comes from one 60 s stream per fold (2 min in total), so 1 alarm = 0.5 FA/min in the table. "0.0" means no alarm in 2 minutes.
  • One seed, 2 folds. The throw-detector branch saw up to 0.25 recall variance between seeds of one config. Differences under about 10 clips (5 %) between runs are noise.
  • Threshold grid stops at 0.99. The old recipe could not reach low FA within it; a hold-frames rule or a finer grid might.
  • Final models are not evaluated themselves. Thresholds transfer from CV only approximately (the throw-detector branch saw this too).
  • The own idle data is one recording. Its two halves differ a lot: people walking in fold 0, quiet in fold 1. All own FA numbers depend on it.
  • "Ordinary sessions" is a post-hoc split (215305 and d01 removed). It is reported because the overall number is dominated by 215305 (64 of 181 clips).
  • The DailyDVS motion distribution is not ours. 47 % of positives are lateral views and motion is wider. Probe 0.58 means "hard to tell", not "identical".
  • Nothing is committed. The data-pipeline worktree has other people's uncommitted changes, which were not touched.

12. Open questions and next moves

For discussion with the team, in my order of expected value:

  1. Record more of our own data in the demo setup. This is the one thing every line of work points to. Interleave throws and feints, label from a phone video or a spoken note (not key presses), and record 5–10 min of idle with people walking, waving and reaching. Session 215305-style labels cap every model at about 10–28 %.
  2. Make own hard negatives explicit. Our idle has walking people but no arm actions. The DailyDVS results show arm actions (punch, wave, point) are what fires a throw detector once the domain shortcut is gone. A minute of us waving and pointing would test this on our camera.
  3. Use the matched external data for pretraining, not mixing. Train throw-motion detection on matched DailyDVS (pos + neg, 47 people), then fine-tune on our data with the r2 recipe. That keeps the generality (it detects other people's throws) without splitting capacity during the final training.
  4. Better readout before more training. Add the throw-detector branch's hold-frames rule and thresholds above 0.99 to evaluate(), then compare r2_old_fa and r2_ext_fa at exactly equal FA.
  5. Multi-seed runs of the two r2 recipes (3 seeds × 2 folds, about 25 min on the A100) before trusting the ranking.
  6. Bring the throw-detector models onto the same evaluation. Run their A–E models through evaluate(), or the r2 models through their replay.py on the 01 stream, so both lines of work are comparable.
  7. Video sources only with high frame rate. Phone slow motion (≥ 120 fps) through v2e_convert.py --up 4, or Kinetics clips at 60 fps. 30 fps video stays recognisable after interpolation.
  8. Earlier goal (real vs. fake), if revisited: make the ball visible (larger, brighter, calm background) and record interleaved takes. The one-feature "ball leaves the top rows" probe (0.91 across sessions) beat every network.
